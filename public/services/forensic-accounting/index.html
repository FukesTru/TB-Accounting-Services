Forensic Accounting

Forensic accounting services in San Jose

Forensic accounting is the detailed review of financial records to find, measure and explain what happened to money, usually for a dispute, a claim or a legal matter.

If you suspect a problem

What should you do first if something looks wrong in the records?

The first days matter, because records can be changed or lost. These steps protect the evidence and your options.

This is general information. Talk with your attorney about your own situation.

  1. Keep the records as they are

    Do not delete, edit or tidy up files, emails or accounting entries. Keep paper records together.

  2. Secure access

    Note who can reach the bank accounts and accounting software, and talk with your attorney about whether access should change.

  3. Write down what you know

    List the dates, amounts and people involved while they are fresh in your mind.

  4. Talk to an attorney before you confront anyone

    An attorney can advise on your rights and the right order of steps.

  5. Call us for a free consultation

    We talk through what happened and whether forensic accounting can answer your questions.

Common matters

When do people need forensic accounting?

These are common reasons people ask for forensic accounting. Tell us about your matter and we will tell you whether we can help.

Business partner disputes

Partners disagree about profits, draws, capital accounts or what the business is owed.

Suspected theft or fraud

Money appears to be missing, or payments look wrong, and the losses need to be measured.

Divorce involving a business

Business income, assets or owner benefits need to be reviewed and documented.

Insurance and loss claims

A business needs to show what an event cost in lost income or damage.

Contract and damages disputes

Lost profits or other damages need to be calculated from the records.

Estate and trust disputes

Beneficiaries or trustees need a clear record of what was received and spent.

The process

How does a forensic accounting engagement work?

Each matter is different, but the work follows the same basic path.

  1. Define the question

    We agree with you, and your attorney if you have one, on exactly what needs answering.

  2. Gather the records

    We list the records needed and keep track of where each one came from.

  3. Analyze

    We rebuild the transactions, compare records against each other and measure any gaps.

  4. Report

    We write up the findings with supporting schedules, in plain language others can follow.

Choosing the right service

How is forensic accounting different from a financial investigation?

The 2 services often work together. The difference is the main question each one answers.

Forensic accounting

Asks what happened and how much it cost. It measures and documents losses, profits or balances so they can support a claim, a settlement or a case.

Financial investigation

Asks where the money went. It follows funds through accounts and transactions to show who received them and how.

Questions

What do people ask about forensic accounting?

Do I need an attorney first?

Not always. Many forensic accounting matters involve an attorney, and the work often runs alongside them. If you do not have one yet, you can still talk through your situation with us on a free consultation.

Will my matter stay confidential?

We keep client records and matters confidential. If an attorney is hiring us for your case, ask them how they want the engagement set up.

What records will you need?

It depends on the question. Bank and credit card statements, the general ledger, invoices, payroll records and tax returns are common starting points. We tell you exactly what to gather once we understand the matter.

Can your findings be used in court?

Our reports are written so attorneys, courts and other parties can follow them. Whether a report is used in court is up to you and your attorney. If testimony may be needed, read about our Expert Witness Services.

How long does forensic work take?

It depends on the number of records and the questions asked. We give you an estimate after the first review of the records.
