The role

What does a controller do?

A controller makes sure the numbers are complete, correct and closed on schedule, every month.

A financial controller oversees the accounting work, closes the books each period and stands behind the financial statements. In a large company it is a full-time job.

Most small and mid-sized businesses need that oversight but not a full-time salary. Our controller services give you the close, the review and the reporting for the time your business actually needs.

Signs it is time

When does a business need controller services?

Businesses usually bring in a controller when the books are being kept, but nobody is checking them.

Talk to us about it
  • The month-end close drags on for weeks, or does not happen at all.
  • Your bookkeeper does good work, but nobody reviews it.
  • A lender, investor or board wants statements they can rely on.
  • You have added locations, entities or project-based jobs.
  • Errors keep turning up at tax time.
  • You want written policies for approvals, payments and reconciliations.

The monthly close

How does a month-end close work?

A close turns a month of transactions into financial statements you can rely on. These are the main steps.

  1. Reconcile every account

    Bank, credit card, loan and payroll accounts are matched to their statements.

  2. Record adjustments

    Accruals are recorded. An accrual is income or an expense that belongs to this month but has not been paid yet.

  3. Review for errors

    Balances are compared with last month and with the budget, and anything odd is checked.

  4. Report and explain

    The financial statements are finalized and we walk you through what changed.

Choosing the right help

Do you need a controller or a CFO?

Many businesses need both at different times. The difference is the direction each one faces.

Controller

Looks at what has happened. Makes sure the books are accurate, closed on schedule and reported correctly. Sets the controls that keep them that way.

Questions

What do owners ask about controller services?

Can you work with our in-house bookkeeper?

Yes. Controller work often means reviewing and guiding the work of an in-house bookkeeper, so the books they keep are closed and reported correctly each month.

Is this a long-term arrangement?

It can be either. We take on long-term controller work and short-term projects, such as a year-end close or a cleanup before a lender review.

What reports will we get?

You get monthly financial statements, and we can build custom financial reports around the numbers you manage the business by.

What are internal controls?

Internal controls are the checks and approvals that keep money and records safe, such as who can approve a payment and who reconciles the bank account. Simple controls catch errors and make theft harder.

How is a controller different from a bookkeeper?

A bookkeeper records the transactions. A controller checks that work, closes the books each period and makes sure the financial statements are right.
