Financial Investigations

Financial investigation services in San Jose

A financial investigation traces money through bank accounts, transactions and records to show where it came from and where it went. TB Accounting Services in San Jose carries out financial investigations for business owners, attorneys and families when funds are missing, misused or hard to follow, and documents each finding.

What we look into

What questions can a financial investigation answer?

Most investigations start with a single question from this list. Many end up answering several.

Where did the money go?

Following funds from an account to the people or accounts that received them.

Was money taken from the business?

Checking payments, payroll and transfers for amounts that were not authorized.

Are there hidden assets or income?

Looking for accounts, income or property that do not appear in the records given to you.

Were funds used as agreed?

Checking whether loans, investments, trust or estate funds were spent the way they were meant to be.

Did a partner take more than their share?

Comparing draws, distributions and expenses with what each owner was entitled to.

Do the records match the story?

Testing the records against invoices, contracts and statements to see what they support.

Tracing funds

How do we trace funds?

Tracing means following each dollar from where it started to where it ended up. It is careful, step by step work.

  1. Collect the statements

    We gather statements for every account involved, for the whole period in question.

  2. Build a timeline

    Every deposit, withdrawal and transfer goes into a single timeline, in date order.

  3. Follow the transfers

    Money moving between accounts is linked, so a transfer out of an account is matched to the deposit it became.

  4. Match to support

    Transactions are matched to invoices, receipts, contracts and payroll records.

  5. Flag the gaps

    Anything without support, or that does not fit the pattern, is listed for review.

  6. Summarize the findings

    The results are written up with schedules that show the source of every figure.

Before it starts

What should you do before an investigation starts?

A few early steps protect the records and keep your options open.

This is general information. Talk with your attorney about your own situation.

  • Keep every record as it is. Do not delete files, emails or accounting entries.
  • Gather the statements you can reach, such as bank, credit card and loan statements.
  • Write down the dates, amounts and people you are concerned about.
  • Speak with your attorney before you confront anyone or change access to accounts.
  • Call us for a free consultation about what an investigation could answer.

Questions

What do people ask about financial investigations?

Can you get bank records for us?

We work from the records you have or can obtain. Your attorney may be able to request more records through the legal process, and we can tell them which records would help most.

Is this the same as a private investigator?

No. We work from financial records and documents. We do not do surveillance or background checks.

Can you tell if someone committed fraud?

We report what the records show, such as transfers without support or payments that do not match an invoice. Whether something is fraud is a legal question for attorneys and courts.

How much does an investigation cost?

It depends on how many accounts and years are involved and what report you need. We explain the fee after the free consultation, before any work starts.
