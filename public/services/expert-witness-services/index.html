The role

What does a financial witness do in a case?

An expert witness is a person a court allows to give opinions on a technical subject, such as accounting, based on their background and analysis.

In a financial case, that work means reviewing the records, building the analysis and explaining the results so a judge or jury can follow them.

Whether a person can testify as an expert witness is decided by the court. Not every matter needs testimony. Many attorneys first need a consultant who can make sense of the financial evidence and help plan the case.

Types of matters

Which legal matters can financial analysis support?

Any case where money is part of the dispute may need financial analysis. These are common examples.

  • Shareholder disputes
  • Breach of contract and lost profits
  • Fraud and embezzlement matters
  • Family law matters involving a business
  • Trust and estate disputes
  • Insurance and loss claims
  • Disputes over accounting records

What the work includes

What does our expert witness work include?

The scope is set with the attorney for each matter. These are the parts it can include.

Case and document review

We review the pleadings and records and list the financial records the case still needs.

Financial analysis

Damages, lost profits, tracing, income and balances calculated from the records.

Written reports

Reports that set out the method, the sources and the findings in language others can follow.

Deposition and trial support

Support for testimony about the financial findings when the case calls for it.

Consulting support

Help understanding the financial evidence and reviewing the other side's numbers, without a testifying role.

Related forensic work

Forensic accounting and financial investigations that build the foundation for a report.

For attorneys

What should an attorney send us first?

A short summary is enough for the first call. Please do not send confidential documents until we have agreed to the engagement.

  • The names of the parties, so we can check for conflicts
  • A short summary of the case and the financial questions in it
  • Key dates, including discovery and report deadlines
  • The financial records already produced or expected
  • Whether you need a consultant, a testifying witness or both

Independence

Whose side are we on?

We report what the records show, whichever side asks.

An analysis that follows the evidence holds up better under questioning. That independence is what makes the work useful to the attorney who hires us.

Questions

What do attorneys ask before hiring us?

When should an attorney bring in financial help?

Early is better. Bringing us in before discovery closes leaves time to request the right records and build the analysis properly.

Can you help without testifying?

Yes. Some attorneys need help understanding the financial evidence, planning discovery requests or reviewing the other side's numbers, without a testifying role.

Will your opinion favor our side?

Our job is to report what the records show, whichever side asks. An independent, well-supported analysis is more useful to a case than one that leans.

Do you check for conflicts?

Yes. Before we take on a matter, we check the parties against our existing clients and work.
